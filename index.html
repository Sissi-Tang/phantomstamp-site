01 / The Problem

A photo travels everywhere. Its copyright rarely does.

A photographer's shot moves from camera, to editing app, to a brand's ad screen — reshared, recompressed, cropped, screenshotted. Visible watermarks are ugly and easy to crop out; metadata disappears on upload; no watermark at all means no proof of ownership once the image leaves your hands.

03 / Technical Design

How the ID hides — and survives

PhantomStamp combines frequency-domain embedding with a geometric-attack recovery layer, so the watermark is both invisible to the eye and resistant to the ways images actually get mangled online.

3.1

Y-channel only

The image is converted to YCbCr colour space, and only the Y (brightness) channel is modified — the Cb and Cr channels are left untouched, which is why the change is invisible to the human eye.

3.2

DCT watermark embedding

Each 8×8 pixel block is converted to the frequency domain via a Discrete Cosine Transform. A single ID bit is encoded by adjusting the relative height of coefficients (1,2) and (2,1) — invisible, structural, and recoverable.

A repeated 8-16 character creator ID is passed through forward error correction and interleaving first, so the payload can be reconstructed even from a damaged fragment of the image.

3.3

Mid-frequency, adaptive strength

Low-frequency changes are visible to the eye; high-frequency detail is usually stripped by compression. The mid-frequency band is the sweet spot — and local AC energy adjusts embedding strength, going stronger wherever texture can hide it and softer over flat, exposed areas.

3.4

DFT synchronization

Sharing rotates, scales, or crops an image. A tiled 512×512 sync template embedded via Discrete Fourier Transform creates four known spectrum peaks — a hidden compass PhantomStamp checks the whole image against to detect that deformation.

Once the angle and scale are found, sub-pixel float resampling re-aligns the image — keeping floating-point luma through extraction instead of rounding early — before DCT decoding even begins.

3.5

Soft voting, fail-closed

Every ID bit is written into many blocks across the image. On extraction, those repeated votes are combined and checked against the error-correcting code — at least three sync-certified repetitions have to agree before a result is accepted.

If the evidence is weak, PhantomStamp returns nothing rather than a guess.

PhantomStamp embeds invisible copyright signatures into images via a Discrete Cosine Transform, surviving JPEG compression, colour grading, and social-media processing without visible trace. A Discrete Fourier Transform synchronization template handles geometric attacks — rotation, scaling, cropping — by detecting deformation of embedded impulse peaks and realigning before decoding. Hamming(8,4) forward error correction with majority voting reconstructs the payload even from heavily damaged fragments. All processing runs on-device via Swift Concurrency, keeping 4K encoding smooth without blocking the UI.
